\section{Radial Collector Well}

% Describe source of problem
A radial collector well, also called a Ranney well, is a central caisson with several horizontal laterals that radiate outward near the base of an aquifer. The caisson is a watertight shaft that is not screened, so water enters the well only through the laterals, and the laterals give the well a large area of contact with the aquifer. A radial collector well can yield much more water than a vertical well for the same drawdown. This example represents a radial collector well as a single multi-aquifer well in the \mf Multi-Aquifer Well (MAW) Package, with an inactive connection for the caisson and one horizontal connection to each cell a lateral passes through, using the NON\_VERTICAL\_WELLS option. A transient simulation that includes storage in the well compares the head in the well with the laterals represented as horizontal connections and as thin vertical screens.

\subsection{Example Description}

% spatial discretization and temporal discretization
The aquifer is 50 $m$ thick and is represented by one layer of 41 rows and 41 columns that are 25 $m$ wide. Horizontal and vertical hydraulic conductivity are 25 $m/d$ and specific storage is $1 \times 10^{-4}$ $1/m$. Head is 50 $m$ at the start of the simulation and is held at 50 $m$ in the cells around the edge of the domain. Model parameters are summarized in table~\ref{tab:ex-gwf-maw-collector-01}.

The caisson is in the center cell of the domain and four laterals extend 12 cells, 300 $m$, north, south, east, and west from it (fig.~\ref{fig:ex-gwf-maw-collector-well}). The caisson is connection 1, a vertical connection to the center cell over the full thickness of the aquifer. The caisson is not screened, so the CONNECTION\_STATUS setting makes the connection inactive, and the well exchanges water with the aquifer only through connections 2 to 49, one for each of the 48 cells the laterals pass through. The connections of each lateral are numbered in order outward from the caisson. The laterals are horizontal boreholes centered 2 $m$ above the base of the aquifer. The aquifer is confined and represented by a single layer, so the elevation of the laterals places the screens within the layer but does not affect the simulated heads. The well radius is 0.15 $m$, the radius of the laterals, and a filter pack with a hydraulic conductivity of 25 $m/d$ extends to a radius of 0.3 $m$. The MAW Package uses one radius for every connection of a well and calculates storage in the well from the same radius, so the storage represented is that of a borehole the size of a lateral rather than that of the larger caisson. The MEAN conductance equation is used for every connection, because the radial conductance equations assume that flow converges on the well in the horizontal plane, while flow to a horizontal lateral converges in a vertical plane.

With the NON\_VERTICAL\_WELLS option, each lateral connection is specified with a tilt angle of $90^{\circ}$ and a connection length of 25 $m$, the length of the lateral within the cell. The screen top and bottom are the top and bottom of the borehole, 0.3 $m$ apart, and determine the elevations over which the connection is saturated; the conductance of the connection depends on the 25 $m$ connection length. Without the option, the same connections are thin vertical screens 0.3 $m$ long, and the conductance of each connection is calculated from that 0.3 $m$ screen length, 0.012 times the conductance of a horizontal connection.

Three simulations are run. A steady-state simulation with the laterals represented as horizontal connections pumps 25,000 $m^3/d$ from the well. A transient simulation pumps 40,000 $m^3/d$ for 5 $d$ and then stops pumping for 5 $d$, each period divided into 25 time steps that increase by a factor of 1.2, and is run twice, with the laterals represented as horizontal connections and as thin vertical screens. Storage in the well is simulated in the transient simulations.

\subsection{Example Results}

Simulated steady-state head is shown in figure~\ref{fig:ex-gwf-maw-collector-map}. The head in the well is 46.99 $m$, 3.01 $m$ below the starting head and 0.014 $m$ below the lowest head in the aquifer, at the caisson and the first cell of each lateral. Drawdown extends along each lateral to its end, and the head contours are elongated along the laterals, because the well withdraws water from every cell a lateral passes through.

\begin{StandardFigure}{
    Simulated steady-state head for a radial collector well pumping 25,000 $m^3/d$, with the laterals represented as horizontal connections. Contours are labeled in meters. The open circle is the unscreened caisson, which has an inactive connection, red cells are connected to the laterals, and gray cells have a constant head of 50 $m$.
    }{fig:ex-gwf-maw-collector-map}{../figures/ex-gwf-maw-collector-map.png}
\end{StandardFigure}

Simulated head in the well for the two transient simulations is shown in figure~\ref{fig:ex-gwf-maw-collector-head}. With the laterals represented as horizontal connections, the head in the well at the end of pumping is 45.18 $m$, a drawdown of 4.82 $m$, and is 0.022 $m$ below the lowest head in the aquifer. With the laterals represented as thin vertical screens, the head in the well is 32.71 $m$, a drawdown of 17.29 $m$, and is 10.39 $m$ below the lowest head in the aquifer. Of the 12.47 $m$ of additional drawdown with thin vertical screens, 10.37 $m$ is the larger head difference between the aquifer and the well needed to drive the discharge through connections with 0.012 times the conductance, because the unscreened caisson provides no other path into the well, and 2.10 $m$ is the lower head in the aquifer. The thin screens also shift the withdrawal toward the caisson: at the end of pumping the lowest head in the aquifer is 45.20 $m$ with horizontal connections and 43.10 $m$ with thin vertical screens, while the head at the end of a lateral is 45.66 $m$ and 46.42 $m$. Storage in the well raises the head in the well by less than 0.001 $m$ with horizontal connections and by at most 0.026 $m$ with thin vertical screens, in the first time step. The head in the well recovers to within 0.1 $m$ of the starting head 0.93 $d$ after pumping stops with either representation of the laterals.
